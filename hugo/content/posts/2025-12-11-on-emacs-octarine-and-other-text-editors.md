## Workflow {#workflow}

I come from the world of Doom Emacs and Neovim, and I am also a programmer and Linux enthusiast, and so I tend to be particular about the experience of editing text on a computer.

One initial disappointment with the workflow of Octarine is the complete lack of Vim keybindings, as there is not even a toggle for them like there is in Obsidian.

“But why does this matter?” I hear you ask. “Can’t you just use the arrow keys like a normal person?”

No! I mean, yeah, I could, but that wouldn’t be quite as fun would it?

I’ve been using Vim/Neovim basically since I started getting into programming in August of 2024, and since then the I’ve grown quite attached to the way in which you edit text with Vim motions.

[This post](https://www.mattjh.sh/post/vim-motions/) is a nice summary of how to learn them, and why they are useful.

In a [post](https://joshblais.com/blog/why-i-left-emacs-for-neovim), Joshua Blais makes the following statement:

“Vim keys, in my opinion, are the superior way to use a text editor, and that is why I never switched away from them - movement, finding of position, and editing were just more intuitive to me.”

I have heard many make this same claim:

“Vim motions are the superior way to edit text, and if you edit text on a computer **for any reason** you should be using Vim/Vim motions.”

I love that Octarine at least acknowledges the awesome ability to not have to take your hands off the keyboard to do most tasks, but they missed the mark by not allowing users to utilize the best keyboard-based system for editing text to ever exist.
